NEXUS brings engineering knowledge to the point where product decisions are made. From the landing page, teams can discover their registered vocabularies and connected information. The industrial foundation helps distinguish physical parts, design definitions, requirements and activities. The benefit is shared business meaning across teams, while keeping source identities and evidence available for review. This demonstration uses labeled example records; it does not claim customer approval.

Start with an engineering decision: what must we review if this housing changes? Select the intended business record and its source version. 120-210s | 3 | Memory: continuity, retained evidence and context
Each memory use case solves a different problem. Short-term continuity lets the engineer ask a follow-up, such as Does this part have PMI, without repeating the whole product story. The selected housing remains the subject, and the answer returns the recorded tolerance and datum. Long-term recall retains conversation evidence outside the immediate exchange. After reloading and reopening the same business scope, the earlier answer reappears. A saved answer is historical evidence, so its freshness still needs checking. Contextual memory binds the discussion to the selected business identity and ontology scope. Requirement and quality questions therefore stay attached to the intended housing. Reviewed lessons are a separate form of retained knowledge; this recording demonstrates conversation persistence and scoped recall, not automatic model training or an approved engineering lesson. The value is less repeated explanation while keeping the evidence and its limits visible.
